01 / Research

Understanding model behavior beyond the benchmark

My research analyzes multimodal and language models, and the metrics used to evaluate them, to understand what actually drives their behavior rather than what their scores suggest.

In applied work, I built LLM pipelines that extract findings from scientific literature into a knowledge graph, with a second model auditing the first for unsupported claims. In my current work I found that a widely used architectural choice causes retrieval-augmented models to largely disregard the evidence they retrieve — a failure invisible to standard benchmarks, established through ablation and weight analysis.

I want to continue by studying trustworthy scientific information extraction, how to detect and attribute unsupported claims when models verify each other, what makes LLMs reliable enough for healthcare, and how vision-language models use visual evidence when they reason.

02 / Publications

Publications & manuscripts in preparation

Work on diagnosing and evaluating retrieval use in RAG systems.

Under review · WACV 2027

Systematic Diagnosis of Retrieval Utilization Failure in Q-Former–Based Multimodal RAG for Radiology Report Generation

Saad Ur Rehman, Vikram Ravindra

Identified that the Q-Former's fixed 32-query bottleneck fundamentally prevents retrieval utilization in vision-language RAG systems, demonstrated via decoder ablation, weight analysis, and a novel content-ablation methodology showing 92.5% output similarity between medically relevant and irrelevant retrieved context.

In preparation · target NAACL 2027

[Working Title] Generalizing Retrieval-Faithfulness Evaluation Across Multimodal and Text-Only RAG Systems

Saad Ur Rehman, Vikram Ravindra

Extends the content-ablation and length-controlled evaluation methodologies to text-only and cross-domain RAG pipelines, aiming to establish architecture-agnostic diagnostics for whether a generation model is actually grounded in retrieved evidence.
