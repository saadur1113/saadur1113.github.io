About

I research multimodal and language models, with a focus on retrieval-augmented generation, model evaluation, and trustworthy scientific information extraction. I try to understand what drives model behavior rather than what aggregate scores suggest.

In my current work I found that a widely used architectural choice causes retrieval-augmented models to largely disregard the evidence they retrieve, a failure invisible to standard benchmarks. I am also interested in detecting unsupported claims when multiple models verify each other, what makes LLMs reliable enough for healthcare, and how vision-language models use visual evidence when they reason.

Publications

Systematic Diagnosis of Retrieval Utilization Failure in Q-Former–Based Multimodal RAG for Radiology Report Generation

Saad Ur Rehman, Vikram Ravindra. Under review, WACV 2027.

Showed that the Q-Former's fixed 32-query bottleneck prevents retrieval utilization in vision-language RAG systems, using decoder ablation, weight analysis, and a content-ablation method that found 92.5% output similarity between relevant and irrelevant retrieved context.

Generalizing Retrieval-Faithfulness Evaluation Across Multimodal and Text-Only RAG Systems (working title)

Saad Ur Rehman, Vikram Ravindra. In preparation, target NAACL 2027.

Extends content-ablation and length-controlled evaluation to text-only and cross-domain RAG pipelines.
