Research Interests

My research centers on analyzing multimodal and language models, and the metrics used to evaluate them, in order to understand what actually drives their behavior rather than what their scores suggest.

In applied settings, I have built LLM pipelines that extract findings from scientific literature into a knowledge graph, with a second model auditing the first for unsupported claims. In my current work I found that a widely used architectural choice causes retrieval-augmented models to largely disregard the evidence they retrieve, a failure invisible to standard benchmarks and one I established through ablation and weight analysis rather than aggregate metrics.

I want to continue this work by studying trustworthy scientific information extraction, including how to detect and attribute unsupported claims when multiple models verify each other, what makes LLMs reliable enough for healthcare, and how vision-language models use visual evidence when they reason.

Systematic Diagnosis of Retrieval Utilization Failure in Q-Former–Based Multimodal RAG for Radiology Report Generation

Saad Ur Rehman, Vikram Ravindra

Identified that the Q-Former's fixed 32-query bottleneck fundamentally prevents retrieval utilization in vision-language RAG systems, demonstrated via decoder ablation, weight analysis, and a novel content-ablation methodology showing 92.5% output similarity between medically relevant and irrelevant retrieved context.

Saad Ur Rehman, Vikram Ravindra

Extends the content-ablation and length-controlled evaluation methodologies to text-only and cross-domain RAG pipelines, aiming to establish architecture-agnostic diagnostics for whether a generation model is actually grounded in retrieved evidence.
